#### Item 4: Paired Statistical Testing (McNemar Test)
> **Viveka's Review:** *"Add a paired test. 15 entities go from precedes to follows. None go the other way. An exact McNemar test gives p about 6e-5.   You are 100% statistically correct. Comparing two marginal sign tests ($p = 6.62 \times 10^{-5}$ vs. $p = 0.215$) tests whether each distribution differs from a 50/50 binomial null, but does *not* test whether the paired transition between raw and weighted regimes is statistically significant.  
  Because the 15 shifts were an artifact of truncating each entity to its peak week, the numerical transition is withdrawn. However, should longitudinal harvesting (Option A) be executed, we will:
  1. Formally implement `mcnemar_exact_test()` in the comparative pipeline to test discordant pairs ($b$ vs. $c$).
  2. Permanently drop all colloquial phrases such as "below the 50% chance threshold."

#### Item 6: Circularity Between Tier Construction and Citation Testing
> **Viveka's Review:** *"Tiers come from the same GDELT data being tested. Explain why this is not circular."*
- **Triage Status:** **Still Applicable**
- **Detailed Response:**

  **Methodological Defense:** The tier assignment for any given domain $d$ is determined by its aggregate cross-entity coverage breadth $B_d$ and total article volume $V_d$ across the entire 50-entity technological corpus, producing an entity-agnostic proxy for general domain prominence. Crucially:
  1. Tier assignment is **time-invariant** (static over the study window) and does not use or optimize for the temporal timing, ramp dates, or perception onset dates of any individual entity.
  2. A domain's tier reflects its systemic reporting breadth across technology, not whether its coverage of Entity $X$ was early or late.
  We will formally add this non-circularity discussion to the methodology text.
